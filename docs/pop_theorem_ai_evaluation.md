# Pop-theorem assessment and evidence boundary

Current interpretation, 2026-09-05. The active scope is
[DSN-20260905-04](../codex/dsn/dsn-20260905-prototype-consolidation.md).
This document replaces the older active selector roadmap; historical numerical
results remain in the linked artifacts and decision records.

## What the repository supports

Keep rank, overlap, composition and byte accounting as useful instrumentation.
Keep tested adapter mechanics and compact SVD. The repository has not established
that a Pop-derived rule chooses better adapter ranks, or that its learned
controllers migrate capacity better than valid fixed-allocation controls.

The repository's polynomial probes investigate an identity involving the ranks
of polynomial functions of a common matrix. Arbitrary LoRA adapters have not
been shown to satisfy that common-operator construction. Observing different
adapter ranks or a favorable pack result does not bridge that modeling gap.
The legacy `theorem` rank-strategy name aliases the Gram-energy heuristic;
`gram_energy` is the descriptive name for new configurations.

## Evidence, with its limits

| Evidence | What it supports | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rank ledger and polynomial probes | Reproducible algebra/measurement mechanics | A trained quality selector or causal role for the theorem |
| Historical fault-code and Text-to-SQL packs | Recorded local size/quality observations | General model/domain benefit or a clean causal allocation comparison |
| Dynamic gates and dormant-factor operations | Tested changes to active components and compact export | Useful automatic growth, cue-only recall or global memory reduction |
| Capacity-migration development | A reproducible fixture with state/rank audits and explicit failed controls | Controller admission or successful learned return-A |
| Compact SVD measurements | Numerical agreement and lower process peaks on synthetic matrices | Downstream quality improvement or whole-model memory savings |

The [fault-code screen](../codex/evidence/fault_codes_paired_control_screen_seed42.json)
and [Text-to-SQL transfer screen](../codex/evidence/text_to_sql_paired_transfer_screen_seed42.json)
remain historical observations. Their shared random seeds did not make initial
factors identical across different rank shapes and target orderings. Per-example
intervals also do not measure uncertainty across independent training seeds.
Native-versus-transplanted margins therefore cannot be read as established
causal placement or domain-specific effects. Older report passes were not bound
to all the artifact identities now required by proof/resume checks.

The engineering repairs and matched `component-v1` initialization are documented
in [DSN-20260905-01](../codex/dsn/dsn-20260905-correctness-and-experiment-integrity.md).
They make better future comparisons possible; they do not repair historical
training runs after the fact.

## What happens next

The [gradient-agreement development gate](../codex/research/gradient-agreement/development-results.md)
failed despite finite, mechanically valid runs. The next proposed test is a
[dense baseline diagnostic](../codex/dsn/dsn-20260905-baseline-validity-diagnostic.md)
on its stored tasks. Resolve learnability and optimization before comparing
another selector.

A later theorem-specific study would need a concrete mathematical bridge,
matched initialization and training schedules, equal-budget controls and
independent seed-level evidence. Those are prerequisites for a new proposal,
not the current work queue. The [parking lot](../codex/parking-lot.md) records
which tracks can return and what would justify reopening them.
